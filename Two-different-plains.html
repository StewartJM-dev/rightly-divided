Rightly Divided — Article Three

Two Different Plains

The Apocrypha, Gnosticism, and the Gospel That Was Never Hidden

By John Stewart

The oldest question about Scripture is not whether it is true. It is whether God is hiding something. Whether the real truth runs deeper than what the church gave you. Whether there is a layer beneath the layer — older, more authoritative, more illuminating — that the institution buried under centuries of control.

That question did not begin with the internet. It did not begin with the discovery of ancient manuscripts in desert caves. It began in a garden, with a serpent, and a single well-crafted suggestion directed at the first human beings who had ever held God's Word.

Yea, hath God said...? ...ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.

Genesis 3:1, 5 — KJV

The structure of that temptation has never changed. There is something God is withholding. There is a deeper truth beyond what you have been given. Reach for it and you will know more than you were meant to know.

This article is the answer to that temptation — told through history, through honest books that admit what they are, and through the most devastating contrast in all of religious literature. We are going to look at the apocrypha honestly, acknowledge its value where it has value, and examine why none of it stands on the same plain as canonical Scripture. Not because we say so. Because the evidence — including the evidence from the apocrypha itself — demands that conclusion.

The World Between the Testaments

Malachi closes the Hebrew canon around 430 BCE. The next time God speaks through a prophet in the biblical record an angel appears to an elderly priest named Zacharias in the Temple and the old man cannot speak for nine months. Between Malachi's last word and Gabriel's appearance lies four hundred years of silence.

That silence has a history. And that history explains almost everything people find confusing about the apocrypha.

After Malachi, the Persian empire gives way to Alexander the Great. Alexander dies young and his empire fractures among his generals. The Seleucid dynasty takes control of the region including Judea. For a time the Jewish people live under relative tolerance. Then, around 175 BCE, a man named Antiochus IV takes the Seleucid throne. He gives himself the title Epiphanes — meaning God Manifest. His subjects called him Epimanes — the Madman. History has largely agreed with his subjects.

Antiochus did not merely want political control over the Jewish people. He wanted to erase their identity entirely and replace it with Greek culture — a program called Hellenization. What he unleashed was not a military campaign. It was a cultural annihilation.

He built a gymnasium directly next to the Temple in Jerusalem — the symbol of Greek culture and athletic competition. Jewish priests abandoned their Temple duties to compete in Greek athletic games. Young Jewish men underwent painful surgical procedures to reverse their circumcisions so they could compete naked in the gymnasium without revealing their Jewish identity. The Torah was outlawed. The Sabbath was outlawed. Circumcision was outlawed under penalty of death. Mothers who circumcised their sons were executed with their dead infants hung around their necks.

Then Antiochus entered the Temple itself. He sacrificed a pig on the altar. He erected a statue of Zeus in the Holy of Holies. He commanded the Jewish people to eat pork and offer pagan sacrifices. And he began the systematic physical destruction of the text that defined the Jewish people.

The books of the law which they found they tore to pieces and burned with fire. Whoever was found possessing the book of the covenant, or if any one adhered to the law, the decree of the king condemned him to death.

1 Maccabees 1:56–57

The Torah itself was being physically destroyed. The people who carried it were being killed for carrying it. The scribes who copied it. The families who read it. The priests who guarded it. All of them targets of a systematic campaign to sever the Jewish people from the Word that made them who they were.

This is the furnace that produced the apocrypha. Everything written in this period — honest history, wisdom literature, identity literature, and the explosion of pseudepigraphical texts claiming ancient authority — was produced by a people under this kind of pressure. Understanding the pressure does not validate everything produced in it. But it explains it. And understanding it is essential to reading it honestly.

First Maccabees — The Honest Witness

Out of this period comes one of the most remarkable documents in ancient religious literature. First Maccabees — written by an anonymous Jewish historian around 100 BCE — covers the Maccabean revolt of 175-134 BCE. It is considered one of the most historically reliable documents of the entire second temple period. Secular historians treat it as a primary source. Archaeologists have used it to locate sites. The events it describes are independently confirmed in Greek and Roman sources.

It is also transparent about what it is. It names no divine author. It claims no prophetic inspiration. It acknowledges its sources. And three times — in three separate contexts — it makes an admission that changes everything about how we read the entire apocryphal literature.

Three Admissions from First Maccabees

1 Maccabees 9:27

"So there was great distress in Israel, such as had not been since the time that prophets ceased to appear among them."

The historian states plainly what the entire community knew — the prophetic voice had ceased. This is not a peripheral observation. It is a description of the defining spiritual reality of the entire period he is documenting.

1 Maccabees 4:46

"They stored the stones in a convenient place on the temple hill until there should come a prophet to tell them what to do with them."

The Maccabees have taken down the stones of the desecrated altar. They do not know what to do with them. They set them aside and wait. They are waiting for a prophet. They know they do not have one. Their practical decisions are explicitly provisional because they have no prophetic guidance.

1 Maccabees 14:41

"The Jews and their priests decided that Simon should be their leader and high priest forever, until a trustworthy prophet should arise."

Their entire governing arrangement is a placeholder. Their leadership structure is explicitly temporary. They are waiting for what they do not have — a true prophet. The most politically significant decision of the Maccabean period is made with the open acknowledgment that it lacks prophetic authority.

These three admissions, coming from the most historically reliable document of the entire period, constitute the most important statement in all of apocryphal literature. Not because First Maccabees is attacking the other books. But because it is honestly describing the era that produced all of them.

Every apocryphal text falls inside the window that First Maccabees explicitly identifies as a prophetic vacuum. Not one predates Malachi. Not one postdates John the Baptist. The honest historian inadvertently strips prophetic authority from everything written around him.

First Maccabees does not need to attack the other books. It simply tells the truth about the era. And the truth is devastating — if there were no prophets between Malachi and John the Baptist, then nothing written in that period can claim to be Thus saith the LORD. The question is not whether these books contain value. Some of them do. The question is whether they can claim the authority of divine inspiration. And First Maccabees — their most historically reliable contemporary — has already answered that question for us.

Daniel and First Maccabees — The Prophet and the Historian

There is a connection between Daniel and First Maccabees that is worth pausing on — because it is one of the most striking confirmations of prophetic inspiration in the entire biblical record.

The Proof

The Inspired Prophet Predicts What the Honest Historian Confirms

Daniel 11 describes in precise detail a king who will desecrate the Temple, abolish the daily sacrifice, erect an abomination of desolation, persecute the people of God, and exalt himself above every god. Daniel writes this approximately 165 years before the events occur. The king described is Antiochus IV Epiphanes.

First Maccabees — written by a historian who makes no claim to prophetic inspiration — documents every detail of Antiochus's campaign as confirmed historical fact. The desecration of the Temple. The abolition of the daily sacrifice. The statue in the Holy of Holies. The persecution of the faithful. Every point Daniel predicted, First Maccabees confirms.

The book that denies its own prophetic status accidentally proves the book that claimed it. The honest historian becomes the unwitting witness for the inspired prophet. Together they make a case neither one could make alone — and the case they make is that Daniel was telling the truth about something that had not yet happened when he wrote it.

What Edification Means — And Why It Matters

Plain Language Definition

Edification

Edification means being built up — in wisdom, in faith, in understanding, in the fear of God. A book that edifies you makes you a better thinker, a warmer believer, a more careful reader of the world God made.

Jerome — the great scholar who translated the Bible into Latin — said the apocryphal books should be read for edification but not used to establish doctrine. What he meant was this: reading Sirach will make you wiser. Reading First Maccabees will deepen your understanding of the world Jesus walked into. Reading the Prayer of Manasseh will move you toward genuine repentance. These are real benefits. They are worth having.

But edification is not the same as authority. You can be edified by a biography of Spurgeon without treating it as Scripture. You can be edified by church history without treating Eusebius as inspired. Edification builds you up within the framework that Scripture has already established. It does not establish the framework itself.

That distinction — between a book that builds you up and a book that speaks with the authority of God — is one of the most important things you can understand about the apocrypha. The honest books know the difference. And they say so.

What the Silence Produced — Three Categories

The four hundred years between Malachi and Matthew were not empty. The Jewish people were thinking, writing, praying, and fighting. What they produced in that silence falls into three honest categories.

Category One

Honest Literature

Books that write in the tradition of Scripture without claiming to be Scripture. They know what they are.

  • 1 Maccabees
  • 2 Maccabees
  • Sirach
  • Wisdom of Solomon
  • Prayer of Manasseh

Category Two

Identity Literature Under Pressure

Books claiming ancient authority at the exact moment ancient authority was under violent assault.

  • 1 Enoch
  • Book of Jubilees
  • Book of Giants
  • Testament of 12 Patriarchs
  • Life of Adam and Eve

Category Three

Gnostic Literature

Books claiming hidden knowledge available only to the spiritually elite. A different religion in biblical clothing.

  • Gospel of Thomas
  • Gospel of Judas
  • Gospel of Mary
  • Gospel of Philip
  • Nag Hammadi texts

The Hellenistic pressure of Antiochus explains the explosion of identity literature in the second temple period. When the Torah is being burned and the people who carry it are being killed, a community desperate to preserve its identity reaches for texts that claim to go deeper than Moses, older than the Temple, more authoritative than the priests who had been compromised or killed. Whether sincere theological resistance or deliberate fabrication — and it was probably both at different times — the result is the same. Books claiming a status that First Maccabees already told us was unavailable in that era.

Gnosticism — The Ancient Threat and Its Modern Face

Gnosticism is a word most people have heard and few can define. Understanding it is essential — not just as ancient history but because its structure is alive and well today in the very conversations that drive people to the Book of Enoch and the Nephilim.

The word comes from the Greek gnosis — meaning knowledge. Gnosticism was not a single movement but a family of religious ideas that flourished in the first through fourth centuries CE. At its heart was a simple and seductive claim — salvation comes not through faith and repentance and the atoning work of Christ, but through secret spiritual knowledge available only to the enlightened.

Gnostic systems typically taught that the material world is evil — created not by the true God but by a lesser or malevolent being called the Demiurge. In many Gnostic systems the God of the Old Testament is the Demiurge — an inferior creator who trapped divine sparks of light inside physical bodies. The true God is higher, hidden, and accessible only through gnosis — secret knowledge that liberates the divine spark from its material prison.

Jesus in Gnostic teaching came not to die for sin but to bring that secret knowledge. In many Gnostic texts He only appeared to have a physical body and only appeared to suffer on the cross — because a truly divine being could not be contaminated by the evil material world. This teaching is called docetism — from the Greek word meaning to seem.

And salvation — in Gnostic systems — is available only to those capable of receiving and understanding the secret knowledge. The common people following the mainstream church are trapped in ignorance. The Gnostics have what the church is hiding. The real gospel is buried under institutional control and only the enlightened can find it.

The early church recognized this immediately as a different religion wearing Christian clothing. John's Gospel confronts it directly from the opening verse — the Word was made flesh, real flesh, because God actually entered the material world He created and declared good. First John opens with — that which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon and our hands have handled — specifically refuting the claim that Christ was not physically real. Paul writes to the Colossians that all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Christ — not distributed in secret knowledge to the spiritually elite.

Irenaeus of Lyon — writing around 180 CE, just one generation removed from the apostle John through his teacher Polycarp — documented and systematically refuted Gnostic teaching in his work Against Heresies. He was not a political actor protecting institutional power. He was a shepherd who had received the apostolic tradition personally and recognized when something contradicted it at the root.

The Gnostic gospels — Thomas, Judas, Mary, Philip — were not suppressed because the church was afraid of them. They were rejected because they taught a different God, a different Christ, a different salvation, and a different gospel than what the apostles had taught and died for. Communities that knew Paul and Peter personally, that had received the letters and gospels directly, that had watched the apostles die for a specific set of claims — those communities were capable of recognizing a counterfeit.

Now consider the modern face of this ancient threat. The appeal of the Book of Enoch, the Nephilim, and the broader hidden knowledge movement is Gnostic in its structure even when it is not Gnostic in its theology. The message is always the same — there is something deeper than what the church gave you. Something hidden. Something suppressed. Something the institution is afraid of. Something only the truly spiritually curious will find.

That is not a new message. It is the oldest lie in the garden. The serpent was the first Gnostic.

The Open Gospel — The Most Devastating Contrast

Here is where the argument becomes undeniable. Not because we make it — but because the gospel of Scripture makes it for us simply by being what it is.

Gnosticism

Salvation through secret knowledge — gnosis unavailable to most
Available only to the spiritually elite — those enlightened enough to seek beyond what they were given
The material world is evil — God did not truly enter it
Christ only appeared to suffer — real divinity cannot be contaminated by flesh
The church suppressed the real gospel — the truth requires finding what was buried

The Gospel of Scripture

Salvation Through Abundance

Truth is proclaimed — shouted from rooftops, read publicly, copied obsessively
Salvation through faith — available to whosoever will, no initiation required
Available to all — every tribe, tongue, people and nation, the ground level at the cross
The material world is God's creation — declared good, entered by God Himself in flesh
Christ truly suffered — our hands handled Him, He is risen bodily
The church preserved the gospel — at the cost of lives, across fifteen hundred years, for everyone

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 3:16 — KJV

Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28 — KJV

And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.

Revelation 22:17 — KJV

Whosoever will. Not whosoever has achieved the gnosis. Not whosoever has unlocked the cosmology of the Watchers. Not whosoever has found the hidden text the church buried. Whosoever will.

The entire preservation history of Scripture — the Levitical guardianship, the public readings, the obsessive copying, the translation into every language, the martyrs who died rather than surrender a manuscript — all of it was in service of this one scandalous truth. God was not hiding His Word. He was broadcasting it. The whole point of preservation was to make sure that nobody — not a shepherd in Bethlehem, not a philosopher in Athens, not a mechanic in Pennsylvania — could ever be excluded from what God had said.

Gnosticism reserves truth for the elite. The gospel offers it to everyone. One hides. The other shouts from the rooftops. One requires initiation. The other requires only a willing heart. That is not a subtle difference. That is two different religions. And the canonical Scripture — its origins, its preservation, its transmission, its public accessibility across every century and every culture — is the living demonstration of which one tells the truth about God.

Two Different Plains

The apocrypha at its best — First Maccabees recording honest history, Sirach gathering wisdom after the harvest, the Prayer of Manasseh giving voice to genuine repentance — is the writing of faithful men doing their best in the silence. Read them. They have real value. Sirach will make you wiser. First Maccabees will deepen your understanding of the world the Messiah entered. The Prayer of Manasseh will move you toward God.

The apocrypha under pressure — pseudepigraphical texts claiming ancient authority they cannot demonstrate, written in the exact window First Maccabees identifies as a prophetic vacuum — is the writing of a community reaching for something it desperately needed and could not honestly claim. Human. Understandable. Historically explicable. But not Thus saith the LORD.

The Gnostic literature is something else entirely — a different religion, built on the oldest lie, offering secret knowledge as an alternative to the scandalously open gospel that God spent fifteen hundred years making available to everyone.

And the canonical Scripture stands apart from all three — not because we declare it so, but because it has always occupied an entirely different category of reality. Its authors did not apologize for their translations. They did not acknowledge their accounts might be mediocre. They did not set stones aside waiting for a prophet to tell them what the text meant. They wrote with an authority the apocryphal authors themselves never claimed to match — and they wrote a message so open, so available, so scandalously accessible that the only way to miss it is to go looking for something hidden instead.

There is nothing hidden. There never was. The serpent lied in the garden and every Gnostic since has been telling the same lie in a different language.

God so loved the world that He gave. Openly. Publicly. For everyone. That is not the behavior of a God who hides things. That is the behavior of a God who wants to be found.

The gospel was never hidden.
It was read publicly, copied obsessively,
translated into every language,
and died for by ordinary people
who wanted everyone to have it.

Whosoever will.
